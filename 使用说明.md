# BlackGuard Player and Project Guide

This guide describes the bilingual public release of **BlackGuard: The Rice-Field Guardians**. It covers the second-stage spray vehicle, the third-stage countdown and audio, background music, and development/testing notes.

## Stage 2: Two-Bottle Spray Vehicle

The player uses a small vehicle carrying two spray tanks instead of holding a hand sprayer. The blue tank represents the standard treatment; the green tank represents BlackGuard. An on-screen legend identifies both options in Chinese and English.

At the beginning of Stage 2, press **E** (or use the on-screen prompt) to board the vehicle. Drive with **WASD** or the on-screen direction controls. Follow the highlighted treatment point, stop nearby, then press **R** or click **Confirm Spray** to spray. **Space** remains available for exiting the vehicle. Each field has three virtual spray stops. After both fields are treated, the game automatically moves to the sunlight comparison. Stage 3 is unchanged by this vehicle interaction.

## Stage 3: Nine-Second Countdown and Audio

The inspection countdown lasts **9 seconds** and appears as a large, translucent full-screen overlay with prominent numbers, a border, and a progress bar. The overlay does not block movement or controls. Its alarm is intentionally lower-pitched and less sharp than the earlier test sound; its rhythm becomes faster near the end. If time expires, a swarm of bugs appears and plays its sound. The countdown does not replace the game's existing timeout outcome.

When bugs disappear, the game uses the `Workspace.Cow` sound when available, with a short tone as a fallback. Sound behavior can vary with Roblox and device volume settings.

## Radar and Music

Each rice-field radar scan plays a short ping about every 0.6 seconds. Scanning audio stops when the scan ends or a warning is raised. During radar scans, the countdown, the cow cue, or the bug-swarm sequence, background music is ducked from about 0.14 to 0.045 volume and restored afterward. When an instruction panel is open, music is lowered to about 0.055. These are in-game volume settings, not guarantees about the listener's device volume.

Background music is looped from the Roblox Creator Store asset `TutorialMusic_01` (asset ID `130053244401794`). The client retries asynchronous loading; if music is still inaudible, check Roblox and system mute/volume settings.

## Playing and Testing Stage 3

The 9-second limit includes travel and three virtual spray actions. The second treatment point is farther away, so the stage is intended to feel urgent. In Studio, test that the radar ping starts and stops at the right moments, **Go to Inspection** starts the alarm, timeout stops the alarm and triggers the swarm cue, and no runtime errors appear. This does not replace testing all three stages for difficulty and accessibility.

## Roblox Studio Notes for Developers

The published player release starts at Stage 1. For a local Studio test, `ServerScriptService > IGEMGrayboxServer` contains `DEFAULT_START_STAGE`; set it to `3` only in a test copy, then restore `1` before publishing. The Stage 3 duration is controlled by `RESPONSE_SECONDS` in `V5DeviceController`; keep any client-side timer labels synchronized with the server value. The formal bilingual release uses 9 seconds.

The game disables character appearance loading to reduce startup delay. Players therefore use the default gray avatar rather than waiting for clothing and accessories to load. This trades avatar customization for a faster start and does not remove the game stages.

## Release Scope

This public repository contains the formal bilingual release and this guide. Development backups, intermediate test builds, and separate source/build scripts mentioned during development are not included. Keep local backups before editing a place file. The public release itself is intended for players; Studio-only settings above are for development and testing.
